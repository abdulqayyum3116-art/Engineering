## Pick a question before a data source

Start with a field decision: where to inspect, whether a reading needs checking or which area needs closer attention. Write down what the team can actually do differently once it has the information.

A map can be detailed without being useful. Before adding a source, ask whether its timing and coverage match the decision. Information that arrives after a task is complete may still help a season review, but it serves a different purpose from an alert.

## Keep the field context

Connect observations to a consistent field identifier and record when and how they were collected. Crop stage, recent work and local conditions can change how a reading should be interpreted.

Sensor readings and field imagery data can sit alongside manual observations. Keep the original source and collection time visible. Avoid presenting a combined view as equally current when some inputs are older or incomplete. A field check may be needed before an unusual pattern becomes a reason to act.

## Make uncertainty visible

Decide what the system should show when a reading is missing, a source is unavailable or coverage is incomplete. An empty area should not imply that conditions are normal.

Agree who reviews an alert and what information they need to investigate it. A useful report can explain the observation, its limits and the next check without prescribing an action that the data cannot support. Interpretation should remain grounded in the operation and the judgement of the people responsible for the crop.

## Start with a bounded trial

Choose a manageable set of fields and one decision to support. Agree how records will be checked and how the team will note the action taken. Review whether the information arrived in time, whether it was understandable and whether it changed the next step.

Use that review to decide which sources belong in the system. The purpose is a reliable decision process with clear ownership. More data is useful only when the team can interpret it and has a reason to use it.
