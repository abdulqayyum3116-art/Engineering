## Follow a task through the day

Choose one routine task and watch how its information moves. Who records it, where do they write it and who needs it next? A farm record system should fit this handover before it adds dashboards or lengthy forms.

List the records that already exist and the problems they cause. Repeated entry, unclear field names and missing dates need different fixes. Keep the first scope focused on a few records the team uses regularly.

## Agree names and ownership

Use consistent identifiers for fields, inputs and tasks. Decide who can create or change those records and who checks corrections. Preserve a history where it matters so an edited entry does not silently replace the original account.

For each form, separate essential information from details that can wait. A task completion record might need a field, date, person and note, while a stock movement needs an item, quantity and unit. Agree those requirements with the people doing the work.

## Design for the working conditions

Test on the devices the team expects to use and in the places where they enter records. Check whether labels are clear, controls are usable and connectivity is dependable.

If offline entry is needed, scope it explicitly. Agree how records sync, how duplicate entries are detected and what happens when two people edit the same item. Do not assume that a web form continues working without a connection.

## Move records in stages

Choose which existing data is worth transferring and check a sample before importing more. Keep the source available during review. A new system cannot resolve an ambiguous record without someone deciding what it means.

Introduce the first workflow with clear ownership and a simple way to report problems. Avoid a critical seasonal handover for rollout where possible. Review the first entries with the team, correct confusing fields and agree when the new system becomes the shared record. Expansion should follow a working routine that people understand.
